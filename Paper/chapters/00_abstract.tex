\section*{Abstract}
Statistical mechanics describes how macroscopic regularities emerge from the
interactions of many microscopic degrees of freedom. We apply this framework to
a closed economy in which $N$ agents exchange a conserved quantity, money, through
random pairwise transactions, in analogy with energy exchange between particles
in a gas. Using agent based simulations, we study how the stationary money
distribution depends on the transaction rules and on the boundary conditions.
When transactions are random and reversible, the distribution relaxes to the
Boltzmann--Gibbs exponential law, $P(m)\propto e^{-m/T}$, with an effective
temperature $T=M/N$ equal to the average money per agent. Allowing agents to
borrow up to a maximum debt $m_d$ leaves the exponential form unchanged but shifts
the lower boundary and raises the temperature to $T_d=m_d+M/N$. When debt is
limited by a required reserve ratio, the distribution becomes double-sided, with
separately conserved positive and negative money. In contrast, when agents
transfer a fixed fraction of their money, detailed balance is broken and the
stationary distribution deviates from the exponential, taking a Gamma like form.
Finally, a multiplicative stochastic model of wealth, in which prices fluctuate
and total wealth is not conserved, leads to a power-law tail at high wealth.
These results show that conservation laws and the (ir)reversibility of the
transaction rules, rather than the details of individual behaviour, determine the
shape of wealth distributions and the emergence of inequality.

\noindent \textbf{Keywords:} Statistical mechanics, Boltzmann-Gibbs distribution, Gibbs entropy, Agent-based simulation, Markov processes, Economic dynamics, Complex systems.
